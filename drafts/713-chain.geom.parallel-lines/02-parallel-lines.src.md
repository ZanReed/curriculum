## Lesson {checkpoint}

Two [[parallel lines]] go in the same direction and never meet. On a diagram, matching arrows on two lines show that they are parallel. A line that crosses them both is a [[transversal]].

Here is the new step. A transversal crosses two parallel lines at the same slant. So the four angles where it meets one line are copied exactly where it meets the other: each angle has the same size as the angle in the same position at the other crossing. Here is Ben's exercise book.

@@FENCE a2-w1@@

```worked
title: Find all eight angles
Start at the top crossing, Q. Angle n is across from the 50° angle. They are [[vertically opposite angles]], so n = 50°.
Angle m sits beside the 50° angle on the straight line CD, so m = 180° − 50° = 130°. Angle r sits beside the 50° angle on the transversal, which is a straight line too, so r = 130°.
Now the bottom crossing, P. The arrows show CD and AB are parallel, so the transversal crosses AB at the same slant. The bottom crossing is a copy of the top one.
s is in the same position at P as the 50° angle is at Q: above the line, right of the transversal. So s = 50°.
In the same way, t is in m's position, u in n's and v in r's. So t = 130°, u = 50° and v = 130°.
Four of the angles are 50° and four are 130°. Ben was right about four of his eight.
```

Usually you need only one angle. Copy first, then use a link you already know.

## Check for understanding {checkpoint}

@@FENCE a2-d1@@

```shortanswer
prompt: CD and AB are [[parallel lines]]. (1) Find x, and explain how you found it. (2) Rawiri says, "Where a transversal crosses two parallel lines, any sharp angle and any wide angle add to 180°, even at different crossings." Is Rawiri right? Explain.
starter: (1) x = …
rubric: Finds x | 1 | x = 55°
rubric: Explains the method | 2 | Links two steps: copies the 125° to the same position at Q because the lines are parallel, then uses a link from activity 01 (x is beside it on the transversal, so 180° − 125° = 55°). Any correct two-step route earns this line.
rubric: Judges Rawiri's claim | 2 | Rawiri is right, and the reason covers every diagram, not just one: the lines are parallel, so all the sharp angles are copies of one size and all the wide angles are copies of one size; at each crossing a sharp and a wide angle sit side by side on a straight line, so those two sizes add to 180°
answer: (1) x = 55°. The arrows show CD and AB are parallel, so the 125° angle at P is copied to the same position at Q: above CD, left of the transversal. x sits beside that angle on the transversal, a straight line, so x = 180° − 125° = 55°.
(2) Rawiri is right. Because the lines are parallel, the bottom crossing is a copy of the top one, so every sharp angle is the same size and every wide angle is the same size. At either crossing, a sharp angle and a wide angle sit side by side on a straight line, so those two sizes add to 180°. So any sharp angle and any wide angle add to 180°, wherever they are, whatever the slant.
solution: Copy the 125° angle to Q, then x is beside it on a straight line: x = 55°. Rawiri is right: with parallel lines there are only two sizes of angle, a sharp one and a wide one, and a side-by-side pair of them adds to 180°.
```

```teacher-guide
## The sequence

One idea: with arrows, each angle is copied to the same position at the other crossing; last lesson's links do the rest. The first example answers the hook: four angles are 50°, four are 130°. The second copies, then takes one step. The third removes the arrows: point out that the copy stops working.

## Watch for

- Students who copy the given angle into every position (`mis.parallel.all-equal`). Ask, "Is x in the same position: above or below, left or right?"
- Students who copy across lines with no arrows (`mis.parallel.assumed`). Ask what in the diagram says the lines are parallel.

## If time runs short

Cut practice item 1, then item 6. Keep every faded example and both check-for-understanding items.

## Marking

- **Finds x (A):** x = 55°.
- **Explains the method (M):** credit any correct two-step chain: a copy, then beside or across. The copy needs a reason (the arrows).
- **Judges Rawiri's claim (E):** credit a reason true for any diagram: parallel lines give two sizes, and a sharp and a wide side by side make 180°. "50° + 130° = 180°" alone doesn't earn this line. Mark the reasoning, not the writing.
```
