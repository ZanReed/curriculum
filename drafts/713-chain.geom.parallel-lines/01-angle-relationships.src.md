## Lesson {checkpoint}

When lines meet, the angles they make are linked. If you know the link, you can find an unknown angle without measuring it.

Here is the first link. A straight line is a half turn, which is 180°. So angles that sit side by side on a straight line add to 180°. Angles side by side like this are [[adjacent angles]], and two angles that add to 180° are [[supplementary angles]].

@@FENCE a1-w1@@

```worked
title: Find angle x
The 140° angle and x sit side by side on the straight line AB. They are [[adjacent angles]] on a straight line, so together they make 180°.
$$140^\circ + x = 180^\circ$$
x is whatever is left of 180° after the 140°.
$$x = 180^\circ - 140^\circ = 40^\circ$$
Check: 140° is a wide angle, so the angle next to it on the line should be narrow. 40° is narrow. So x = 40°.
```

Now a second straight line goes through O. Two straight lines cross, and they make four angles.

@@FENCE a1-w2@@

```worked
title: Find angles y and x
Start with y. y and the 40° angle sit side by side on the straight line AB, so they add to 180°.
$$y = 180^\circ - 40^\circ = 140^\circ$$
Now x. x and y sit side by side on the other straight line, CD, so they add to 180° too.
$$x = 180^\circ - 140^\circ = 40^\circ$$
So x = 40°: the same size as the angle across from it, not the widest one.
That isn't luck. x and the 40° angle are each what's left of 180° after y, so they must be equal. Angles across from each other where two lines cross are [[vertically opposite angles]], and they are always equal.
```

The last link is about [[right angle]]s. Two angles side by side that fill a right angle add to 90°. They are [[complementary angles]].

@@FENCE a1-w3@@

```worked
title: Find angle x
The square at O marks a [[right angle]]. The line OC splits it into two parts: 35° and x.
The two parts fill a right angle, not a straight line, so they add to 90°, not 180°.
$$35^\circ + x = 90^\circ$$
$$x = 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$$
Check: 35° + 55° = 90°. So x = 55°.
```

```callout
variant: note
Name the link first. Side by side on a straight line: they add to 180°. Side by side filling a right angle: they add to 90°. Across from each other where two lines cross: they are equal.
```

```teacher-guide
## The sequence

One idea: name the link, then the unknown is either equal to a known angle or what's left of a known total. The first example sets up the straight line: side by side, the angles make 180°. The second adds a second line through the same point and uses that same step twice. Point out the result: the angle across from 40° is 40°, not the widest one. That answers the squashed-X hook. The third keeps the same steps but changes the total to 90°, because the square marks a right angle.

## Watch for

- Students who give the angle across a crossing as 180° minus the known one (`mis.angles.vertical-as-supplementary`). Ask them which line the two angles share. They share none.
- Students using 180° inside a right angle, or 90° on a straight line (`mis.angles.complement-supplement-swapped`). Ask, "Is there a square?"
- Students who make adjacent angles equal (practice item 4). Ask whether x is across from the known angle or beside it.
```
