## Lesson {checkpoint}

A [[composite shape]] is made of simpler shapes joined together. To find its [[area]], split it into rectangles and triangles that don't overlap. Find any missing length, find the area of each piece, then add the pieces.

Here is an L-shaped deck. Each of its arms is 2 m wide.

@@FENCE a3-w1@@

```worked
title: Find the area of the deck
Split the deck along the dashed line. That makes two rectangles that don't overlap: a bottom one and a top one.
The bottom rectangle is 6 m long and 2 m wide.
$$6 \times 2 = 12$$
The top rectangle is 2 m wide. Its height is missing. The whole left side, FA, is 5 m, and the bottom rectangle uses 2 m of it, so the top one is 5 − 2 = 3 m tall.
$$2 \times 3 = 6$$
Add the pieces: 12 + 6 = 18. The area is 18 m².
Rangi worked out 6 × 2 = 12 and 5 × 2 = 10, and ordered 22 m² of decking. His two rectangles both include the 2 m by 2 m corner, so he counted it twice: 22 − 4 = 18. Split so the pieces don't overlap.
Cut the other way, straight down from D, and you get 2 × 5 = 10 and 4 × 2 = 8: 18 m² again. Any split works if the pieces don't overlap.
```

This shape is a rectangle with a triangle on top.

@@FENCE a3-w2@@

```worked
title: Find the area of shape ABCDE
Split along the dashed line EC: a rectangle ABCE underneath, and a triangle CDE on top.
The rectangle is 8 cm by 4 cm.
$$8 \times 4 = 32$$
The triangle's [[base]] is EC, 8 cm. Its [[height]] is missing. The whole shape is 7 cm tall, and the rectangle uses 4 cm of that, so the triangle is 7 − 4 = 3 cm tall. CD is 5 cm, but it leans, so it isn't the height.
$$\tfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 3 = 12$$
Add the pieces: 32 + 12 = 44. The area is 44 cm².
```

```mc
prompt: Sione finds the area of this L-shape. He works out 7 × 2 = 14 and 3 × 7 = 21, and gives 14 + 21 = 35 cm². What is Sione's error?
(x) His two rectangles overlap. The 3 cm by 2 cm corner is in both. The top rectangle is only 3 × 5 = 15, so the area is 29 cm².
( ) There is no error. 14 + 21 = 35. :: The sums are right, but the 3 cm by 2 cm corner is in both rectangles, so it is counted twice. :: mis.area.composite-overlap
( ) Sione should multiply the outside lengths: 7 × 7 = 49 cm². :: 7 × 7 is the square around the whole shape. It includes the empty corner, which isn't part of the shape.
solution: Split so the pieces don't overlap: 7 × 2 = 14 at the bottom, and the top rectangle is 3 cm wide and 7 − 2 = 5 cm tall, so 3 × 5 = 15. 14 + 15 = 29 cm².
```

```teacher-guide
## The sequence

One idea: split into pieces that don't overlap, find any missing length, find each area, add. The first example answers the deck hook: 18 m², and Rangi's 22 counts the corner twice. Show both cuts, so students see any split works. The second changes one thing: the top piece is a triangle, and its height is a missing length (the whole height take away the rectangle's), not the slanted side.

## Watch for

- Rectangles that overlap at the corner (`mis.area.composite-overlap`). Ask students to shade each piece in a different colour: no square may be shaded twice.
- Taking a whole outside length as a piece's length. Ask, "How much of that side does this piece use?"
- The slanted roof edge used as the height (`mis.area.slant-as-height`), or the roof not halved (`mis.area.triangle-no-half`).
```
