## Lesson {checkpoint}

The [[perimeter]] of a shape is the total distance around its outside edge. To find it, go once round the edge and add up the length of every side. A [[polygon]] is a flat shape with straight sides, so its perimeter is the sum of its side lengths.

@@FENCE a1-w1@@

```worked
title: Find the perimeter of the patio
Each paving stone is a square, 1 m long on each side. The perimeter is the distance round the outside, so follow the outside edge and add up the stone edges you pass.
Along the top: 6 m. Down the right side: 3 m. Along the bottom: 6 m. Up the left side: 3 m.
$$6 + 3 + 6 + 3 = 18$$
The perimeter is 18 m.
Counting the stones that touch the edge gives 14, not 18. A corner stone has two outside edges, one on each side of the corner, so counting stones misses 1 m at every corner. Count edges, not stones.
```

Now there's no grid, and only two sides have labels.

@@FENCE a1-w2@@

```worked
title: Find the perimeter of rectangle ABCD
ABCD is a rectangle, so its opposite sides are equal. The top, CD, is 8 cm, like the bottom. The right side, BC, is 5 cm, like the left.
Go round and add all four sides.
$$8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26$$
The perimeter is 26 cm.
A shortcut: one length and one width take you halfway round, so double them.
$$2 \times (8 + 5) = 2 \times 13 = 26$$
A square has four equal sides, so its perimeter is 4 times one side. That works backwards too: a square with a perimeter of 20 cm has sides of 20 ÷ 4 = 5 cm.
```

This shape has a corner cut out. Two of its sides have no length yet.

@@FENCE a1-w3@@

```worked
title: Find the perimeter of the L-shape
This L-shape is a [[composite shape]]: two rectangles joined. Find the two missing sides first, from the sides opposite them.
Across: the bottom, AB, is 10 cm. Along the top, EF and the side marked a cover the same distance across between them. So a = 10 − 4 = 6 cm.
Up and down: the left side, FA, is 7 cm. On the right, BC and the side marked b cover the same height between them. So b = 7 − 4 = 3 cm.
Now add all six sides, in order round the shape, so none is missed.
$$10 + 4 + 6 + 3 + 4 + 7 = 34$$
The perimeter is 34 cm.
```

```callout
Go once round the edge and add every side. If a side has no length, find it first from the side opposite it. Count edges, not squares.
```

```teacher-guide
## The sequence

One idea: go once round the edge and add every side, finding any missing side first from the side opposite it. The first example answers the patio hook: count stone edges, not stones. Point out that the corner stones are where 14 and 18 part company. The second takes the grid away, so the rectangle's opposite sides supply the missing lengths. Say the shortcut aloud: double the length plus width, added first. The third cuts a corner out: the missing sides come from the long sides opposite them.

## Watch for

- Counting the squares along the edge (`mis.perimeter.counts-squares`). Ask, "How many outside edges does the corner stone have?"
- Adding only the labelled sides of an L-shape. Ask students to touch each side as they add it.
- Students who judge a perimeter by one long side or by the number of sides (practice item 6). Have them add every side. If anyone asks whether the two shapes cover the same amount of ground, leave it open: the next lesson's opening question asks exactly that.

Cut practice item 2, then item 5. Keep every faded example and both check-for-understanding items.
```
