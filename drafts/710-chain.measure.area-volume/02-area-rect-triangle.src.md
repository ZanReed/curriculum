## Lesson {checkpoint}

Perimeter measures the distance round a shape. A shape's [[area]] measures something else: how much flat surface it covers. We measure area by counting [[square unit]]s. A square 1 cm long and 1 cm wide is one square centimetre, written 1 cm². A square 1 m by 1 m is one square metre, written 1 m².

@@FENCE a2-w1@@

```worked
title: Find the area of the rectangle
Each small square is 1 cm by 1 cm, so each one is 1 cm².
There are 2 rows, with 7 squares in each row.
$$2 \times 7 = 14$$
The area is 14 cm². Rows times the squares in a row is the same as length × width, so you don't need to count one by one.
$$\text{area of a rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$$
Compare its perimeter: 7 + 2 + 7 + 2 = 18 cm. A perimeter is a length, in cm. An area counts squares, so it is in cm².
```

A school māra kai, a food garden, has 20 m of fencing. Here are two plans for it.

@@FENCE a2-w2@@

```worked
title: Same fence, same garden?
Both plans use 20 m of fence: 9 + 1 + 9 + 1 = 20 and 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20.
Now find the area of each, in square metres.
$$9 \times 1 = 9 \qquad 5 \times 5 = 25$$
Plan 1 gives 9 m² of garden. Plan 2 gives 25 m², nearly three times as much.
Same perimeter, different area. Knowing the perimeter doesn't tell you the area.
```

Now a [[right-angled triangle]]. It fills exactly half of the rectangle drawn around it.

@@FENCE a2-w3@@

```worked
title: Find the area of triangle ABC
The [[diagonal]] AC cuts the dashed rectangle into two matching triangles, so triangle ABC is half the rectangle.
The rectangle is 7 × 6 = 42 cm². Half of that is the triangle.
$$\tfrac{1}{2} \times 7 \times 6 = 21$$
The area is 21 cm².
The side the triangle sits on, AB, is the [[base]]. The [[height]] is the distance from the base to the top corner, measured at a [[right angle]] to the base: here, BC.
$$\text{area of a triangle} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$$
```

In this triangle, no side meets the base at a right angle.

@@FENCE a2-w4@@

```worked
title: Find the area of triangle ABC
The base is AB, 10 cm. The height is the dashed line from C down to AB. The small square shows it meets the base at a right angle. It is 12 cm.
BC is 13 cm, but it isn't the height. It leans, so it doesn't meet the base at a right angle.
The dashed height splits the triangle into two right-angled triangles. Each is half of its own rectangle, so the whole triangle is still half of a 10 cm by 12 cm rectangle.
$$\tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 12 = 60$$
The area is 60 cm². Always use the height that meets the base at a right angle, never a slanted side.
```

```callout
variant: note
Area counts squares, so its unit is squared: cm² or m². A rectangle: length × width. A triangle: ½ × base × height, where the height meets the base at a right angle.
```

## The sequence

One idea: area counts square units, a rectangle is rows times columns, and a triangle is half the rectangle around it, using the height at right angles to the base. The first example is quick: say the contrast with its perimeter aloud. The second answers the māra kai hook: same fence, 9 m² against 25 m². The third shows the triangle as half its rectangle. The fourth changes one thing: no side stands at right angles to the base, so the height is the dashed line, not the slanted side.

## Watch for

- Area answers in cm or m (`mis.units.area-as-linear`). Ask, "Does that count squares or measure a length?"
- Using the slanted side as the height (`mis.area.slant-as-height`). Ask students to find the small square first.
- Forgetting the half (`mis.area.triangle-no-half`). Ask, "What rectangle is this triangle half of?"
- Students typing ². Any of cm2, cm^2, sq cm or square centimetres is accepted. Tell them before practice.

## If time runs short

Cut practice item 1, then item 4. Keep every faded example and both check-for-understanding items.
