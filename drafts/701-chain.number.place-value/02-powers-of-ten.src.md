# Multiplying and Dividing by 10, 100 and 1000

```objectives
title: Today's goals
Multiply a decimal by 10, 100 or 1000 by moving each digit to a bigger place
Divide by 10, 100 or 1000 by moving each digit to a smaller place
Spot the error of writing a zero on the end of a decimal
```

## Review

From memory: which is larger, 0.6 or 0.58? {{=0.6 | !0.58 :: Line up the points: 0.60 and 0.58. Tenths first: 6 tenths beats 5 tenths. :: mis.place-value.longer-is-larger}}

## Lesson {checkpoint}

Each place is worth ten times the place on its right: ten tenths make one, ten ones make a ten, ten tens make a hundred. So when you multiply by 10, every [[digit]] becomes worth ten times as much, and moves one place to the left. The [[decimal point]] stays where it is. The digits move past it.

```worked
title: 0.3 × 10
0.3 is 3 tenths.
Times 10, each tenth becomes a one, so 3 tenths become 3 ones.
The 3 moves one place left, from the tenths place to the ones place.
0.3 × 10 = 3.
Not 0.30. A 0 on the end doesn't change a decimal: 0.30 is still 3 tenths.
```

```worked
title: 3.4 × 10
3.4 is 3 ones and 4 tenths.
Times 10, every digit moves one place left. The 3 ones become 3 tens, and the 4 tenths become 4 ones.
3.4 × 10 = 34.
```

Now times 100: ten times ten. Every digit moves two places left.

```worked
title: 3.4 × 100
The 3 ones move two places left, to the hundreds. The 4 tenths move two places left, to the tens.
Nothing is left in the ones place, so it gets a 0 to hold it.
3.4 × 100 = 340.
```

Dividing is the opposite. Every digit becomes worth less, and moves to the right: one place for 10, two for 100, three for 1000.

```worked
title: 34 ÷ 10, then 34 ÷ 100
34 is 3 tens and 4 ones.
Divide by 10: each digit moves one place right. 3 tens become 3 ones, and 4 ones become 4 tenths. 34 ÷ 10 = 3.4.
Divide by 100: each digit moves two places right. 3 tens become 3 tenths, and 4 ones become 4 hundredths. The ones place is empty, so it gets a 0. 34 ÷ 100 = 0.34.
```

```callout
variant: note
Times 10, 100 or 1000: every digit moves 1, 2 or 3 places left. Divide: it moves right. Fill an empty place before the point with 0. Writing a 0 on the end of a decimal doesn't multiply it.
```

```teacher-guide
## The sequence

One idea: times or divide by 10, 100 or 1000, every digit moves to a bigger or smaller place, and the point stays put. The first example is the card-stack hook: 0.3 × 10 = 3, not 0.30. Then 3.4 × 10 and 3.4 × 100: one place, then two, and an empty place filled with 0. The last example turns it round with the same digits: 34 ÷ 10 and 34 ÷ 100.

## Watch for

- A zero written on the end of a decimal (3.4 × 10 = 3.40). Ask, "Is 3.40 bigger than 3.4?"
- Moving the wrong number of places. Ask, "How many zeros: so how many places?"
- Moving left for divide. Ask whether the answer should be bigger or smaller.
```
