## Lesson {checkpoint}

A [[net]] is a flat shape that folds up to make a solid. Each flat surface of the solid is a [[face]], and a line where two faces meet is an [[edge]].

To check whether a net makes a solid, do three things. First, name the faces the solid needs: how many, and what shapes. Second, check that the net has exactly those faces. Third, fold it in your head: put one face down as the bottom and fold the others up around it. Every face must land in its own place, and sides that meet when you fold must be the same length.

@@FENCE a1-w1@@

```worked
title: Does this net fold into a cube?
A [[cube]] needs six square faces. The net has six squares, so the faces are right. Now fold it.
Put square 2 on the desk. It is the bottom.
Squares 5 and 6 fold up on its left and its right. They are the left and right faces.
Square 1 folds up behind square 2: the back. Square 3 folds up in front: the front.
Square 4 is joined to square 3, so it folds over the top: the lid.
Every square landed in its own place: bottom, left, right, back, front and lid. So the net folds into a cube.
```

The same six squares, arranged differently.

@@FENCE a1-w2@@

```worked
title: Does this net fold into a cube?
Six squares again, so the faces are right. Now fold it.
Put square 1 on the desk as the bottom. Square 2 folds up as the right face.
Square 3 folds over as the lid, and square 4 folds down as the left face.
Square 5 folds under and lands on square 1, the bottom. Square 6 lands on square 2.
Two places got two squares each, and the front and the back are still open. So this net does not fold into a cube.
Having the right faces is not enough: each one has to fold into its own place.
```

```callout
variant: warning
Six squares don't always make a cube. Fold them in your head: if two squares land in the same place, it is not a net of a cube.
```

The same three steps work for any solid. Here is a [[prism]].

@@FENCE a1-w3@@

```worked
title: Does this net fold into a triangular prism?
A triangular [[prism]] has two triangles for its ends, joined by three rectangles: five faces.
The net has two triangles and three rectangles, so the faces are right. Now fold it.
Put the middle rectangle on the desk. The left and right rectangles fold up and lean in until their long sides meet. That makes a tube with a triangle-shaped hole at each end.
One triangle is on the top of the middle rectangle and one is on its bottom, so they fold up at different ends and close both holes.
Check the sides that meet: each side of a triangle is 2 cm, the same as the short side of the rectangle it folds against. So the net folds into a triangular prism.
```

The last example: a [[pyramid]].

@@FENCE a1-w4@@

```worked
title: Does this net fold into a square-based pyramid?
A square-based [[pyramid]] has a square [[base]], with a triangle standing on each side of the square: one square and four triangles.
The net has one square, and a triangle on each of its four sides, so the faces are right. Now fold it.
Put the square on the desk as the base. Each triangle folds up from its own side of the square.
The four triangles lean in, and their tops meet at one [[vertex]] above the middle of the square.
Check the sides that meet: the four triangles are all the same, so the slanted sides that meet are the same length. So the net folds into a square-based pyramid.
```

```teacher-guide
## The sequence

One idea: a net works when it has the solid's faces and every face folds into its own place, with matching sides. The first two examples are the minimal pair: six squares that fold, then six that don't (the cross-or-line hook's answer). Students fold a pre-cut sheet of both shapes during those two examples, then fold in their heads.

## Watch for

- "Six squares, so it's a cube" (`mis.nets.any-six-squares`). Ask, "Where does each square land?"
- A pyramid net without its base, or with the wrong number of triangles. Ask how many sides the base has.
- Cuboid nets with a rectangle that doesn't fit the side it folds against.

## If time runs short

Cut practice item 4, then item 2. Keep every faded example and the whole check for understanding.

## Marking

- **Decides about net P (A):** yes.
- **Explains the fold (M):** 2 for every square placed; 1 if some are left out.
- **Judges Wiremu's claim (E):** 2 for "no" with a six-square example that fails, why, and the condition that every square lands on its own face; 1 for an example alone. Mark the reasoning, not the drawing.
```
